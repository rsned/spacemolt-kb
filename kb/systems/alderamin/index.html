Alderamin is Arabic for the right arm, a name for a place that people once reached for and then let go of. Its fast-spinning blue-white star pours out enough ultraviolet to age unshielded optics in a season, and the charts of the place are older than most of the crews using them. Help is many jumps behind you.

☀ Alderamin Star Sun A0V 0.0 (0.0, 0.0) — — A blue-white A-type star that spins so fast its equator bulges, pouring out enough ultraviolet to age unshielded optics in a season. Pilots plot around the glare more than toward it.
● Alderamin I Planet scorched 2.6 (-2.6, -0.0) — — Bare basalt stripped to a glassy sheen where the ultraviolet melted the surface and let it refreeze. Survey drones that held station here came home pitted like old coins.
● Alderamin II Planet arid 5.0 (-2.0, -4.6) — — Pale dust plains and dry salt flats, scoured bright by the star and too harsh for anything to grow. The wind has sorted the grains by size into long, tidy bands.
● Alderamin III Planet terran 6.8 (-5.1, -4.5) — — Hot light this strong should have boiled the seas away, but a thick ozone haze filters the worst of it and leaves cool water under a milky sky. A habitat frame still stands on the coastal shelf, stripped of its panels and facing the sea.
