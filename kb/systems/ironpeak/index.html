☀ Ironpeak Star Sun NS 0.0 (0.0, 0.0) — — The name predates the discovery. Early surveyors catalogued this as a high-iron system and moved on. What they logged as a dim compact object at the center was, in fact, a neutron star — the collapsed remnant of a massive iron-core progenitor. Iron-core collapse is the mechanism. The name is a coincidence. The coincidence is unsettling.
● Ironpeak I Planet scorched 1.0 (1.0, 0.0) — — Tidally locked and bathed in X-ray flux. The dayside has been geologically reset by radiation heating. The nightside is simply cold and dark. Neither hemisphere is hospitable.
○ Ironpeak Weak Superposition Nebula — 2.5 (2.1, -1.4) — — Quantum fragments hang here between present and absent, and the mining beam seems to decide which. One pass fills the hold with fragments, and the next brings up only iron and copper dust.
◈ Strange Matter Node Asteroid Belt — 3.9 (-2.5, 3.0) Yes 90 The rock parts to show a vein of exotic matter set in common stone, cold to every instrument and wrong in weight for its size. It reads as dead until a drill touches it, and then it does not.
◈ Ironpeak Belt Asteroid Belt metallic 4.1 (4.0, -1.0) — — Iron-rich supernova ejecta that never fully accreted. The gold traces here were synthesized in the r-process during the collapse — the neutron star's birth forged heavy elements in microseconds and scattered them across the system. The exotic matter embedded in the belt is harder to explain.
