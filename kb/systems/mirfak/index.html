☀ Mirfak Star Sun NS 0.0 (0.0, 0.0) — — What remains of a star that was once one of the largest in this region. It went supernova — no record of when, no witnesses. What's left is roughly twenty kilometers across and contains more mass than the original star's core. It pulses in X-ray frequencies at 0.847-second intervals. Surveying equipment near the pulse axis gives unreliable readings. The pulse axis is not clearly marked.
◈ Phase-Shifted Deposit Asteroid Belt — 0.0 (0.0, 0.0) Yes 0 Exotic matter that exists partially in another dimension. It flickers when observed directly.
◈ Mirfak Belt Asteroid Belt metallic 3.0 (3.0, 0.0) — — Debris from the supernova that created the neutron star. The exotic matter traces here aren't coincidental — the collapse event compressed ordinary matter in ways that shouldn't be possible and left behind material with anomalous physical properties. Standard assay equipment flags it as interference. It is not interference.
◈ Pulsar Fallout Shoals Asteroid Belt metallic 5.8 (-5.4, 2.1) — — Lead-rich supernova debris circles beyond Mirfak's neutron star, shielding narrow uranium seams from the remnant's relentless X-ray pulse. The field is sparse, dark, and far enough from settled lanes that only deliberate prospectors work it.
