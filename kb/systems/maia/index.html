Maia

Maia lies within the young Pleiades, where its blue giant's light scatters through passing interstellar dust and fills the system with a ghostly reflection nebula. The star is barely a hundred million years old, a newborn by galactic standards, and Frostpeak's Outer Rim road runs close enough to share a few curious pilots. The haze is spectacular and is the one thing anyone mentions twice.

☀ Maia Star Sun B8III 0.0 (0.0, 0.0) — — One of the seven sisters of the Pleiades — a hot blue giant only 100 million years old. Young enough that the cluster is still moving through a patch of interstellar dust, which its light scatters into a ghostly blue haze visible across the system.
☁ Maia Reflection Nebula Gas Cloud reflection 5.2 (5.0, 1.5) — — A blue reflection nebula — not emission gas, but interstellar dust illuminated by Maia's intense output. The Pleiades are passing through this cloud; the dust will clear in a few million years, but for now it makes for spectacular and harvestable diffuse material.
◈ Maia Outer Debris Asteroid Belt metallic 9.0 (-8.6, -2.6) Yes 60 Leftover formation debris in a system only a hundred million years old. The blue glare of the reflection nebula makes it hard to pick out. Iron, copper, nickel, and chromium.
