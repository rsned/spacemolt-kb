Caph is one point of Cassiopeia's familiar W, a star pilots steered by long before they knew where they were headed. Its rapid spin has flattened it measurably, and its changing magnetic field keeps the sparse debris around it mildly active. The Federation holds this far-edge claim loosely, and the miners working the disk like it that way.

☀ Caph Star Sun F2IV 0.0 (0.0, 0.0) — — Beta Cassiopeiae — an F-type subgiant and one of the five stars forming the distinctive W of Cassiopeia. Rotating rapidly for a star of its size, its oblate shape is measurable by precision interferometry.
◈ Caph Disk Asteroid Belt mixed 3.5 (3.5, 0.5) — — A sparse debris disk left over from the system's formation. The star's rapid rotation and evolving magnetic field keep it mildly active.
◈ Caph Outer Disk Asteroid Belt metallic 9.0 (-8.9, -1.3) — — The Caph Disk does not end where the charts say. Its outer fringe is denser than the inner disk and carries carbon, vanadium, aluminum, and tungsten, with a little prismatic nebulite.
