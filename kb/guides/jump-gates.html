Guides / Jump Gates

Jump Gates

What a jump gate is, how the recipes are earned, what the parts take, where the rare inputs come from, and how a gate site is founded. Much is still unmeasured.

A jump gate is a fold in space held open between two systems: a Lane Anchor at each end holds the fold, a lot of Fold Lattice gives it something to stand on, and Gate Regulators keep it from drifting. Players build them. The first gate in the galaxy was built by another player (player-reported), and more are under construction now.

The recipes are not in the public catalog yet. The numbers below come from our own recipe capture after finishing the chain that grants them. As elsewhere in the KB, our own observations are labelled measured, other players' reports player-reported, and the official docs doc-quoted. This guide is young: everything we have not measured is listed as such at the bottom, and the guide grows as we learn.

⚠ Monoliths. The rare inputs come from Frontier Repeaters. The largest kind, the Repeater Monolith, cannot be beaten today: every kill spawns two more, and a fight with them cannot be fled. One pilot fought Monoliths for eight hours, destroyed 354 of them, and was lost (player-reported). Stay out of any system where you have seen one.

⚠ A gate costs money for as long as it stands. Building it pays labor from your faction treasury every facility cycle, and an open gate needs upkeep at both ends. Budget for it the way you would for a station (see the station guide).

The recipes are granted when you complete The Method, the last mission of Sinter's chain. Its completion text sums the build up: "A Lane Anchor at each end, holding the fold. Fold Lattice, a lot of it, to give the fold something to stand on. Gate Regulators to keep it from drifting. The fold wears through its shims, and the shims are seam glass, so whoever builds a gate had better plan to keep feeding it. Every empire's materials and a lot of the frontier's."

Earlier in the chain, Taking the Machine Apart is where the first Fold Knot turns up; the fold knot is the hinge every Lane Anchor is built around.

All five are hand-crafted in a station workshop. Hand crafting charges no labor fee. The times above are ticks per run for the crafter who captured them, already sped up by their skills; yours will differ.

  • Lane Anchor: one at each end of the gate, so two per gate. Each takes a whole Fold Knot, which is the scarcest input in the whole build. Per the recipe note, dismantling a gate returns each fully built end's Lane Anchor.
  • Lane Anchor Prototype: a test build with a fifth of the Seam Glass and mirror facets and a quarter of the Darksteel Plating. It still needs a full fold knot.
  • Fold Lattice: "a lot of it": 40 per end, 80 per gate (see Found the site). Each one uses Seam Glass, echo cells and Phase Matrix.
  • Gate Regulator: keeps the fold from drifting. 10 per end, 20 per gate. Uses echo cells, Solarian Composite and Prismatic Lens.

Put together, the gate parts for a whole gate (2 Lane Anchors, 80 Fold Lattice, 20 Gate Regulators) come to about 900 seam glass, 240 echo cells, 20 mirror facets and 2 fold knots, plus 80 darksteel plating, 800 phase matrix, 200 solarian composite and 100 prismatic lens (computed from the recipes above and the phase lists).

  • Fold Shim: the wear part. The fold wears through its shims, so a standing gate needs a steady supply of Annealed Seam Glass and Circuit Board.

The common inputs (Darksteel Plating, Phase Matrix, Solarian Composite, Prismatic Lens, Circuit Board) are ordinary refined goods. The hard ones are Seam Glass, echo cells, mirror facets and above all the Fold Knot: see the next step.

Repeaters are Frontier hostiles, crystalline machines found in small groups out past charted space. Echo cells, mirror facets and fold knots all come from their wrecks: loot the wreck after each kill. The Fold Knot is an uncommon spire drop (too few kills yet to give a rate), so a gate's two anchors mean a lot of spires.

Repeaters reflect damage. Every hit on a pane sends back a flat amount (about 15) as EM damage; a spire sends back a share of the energy damage it takes. Kinetic weapons are the better choice against spires, and the reflected damage counts against your ship even though the repeater never "fires".

Seam Glass is mined, not crafted. It sits in nebula shells guarded by repeaters: in the deposits we know, every shell of any size had a group sitting on it. Deposit sizes vary widely. We are not publishing where they are; look for nebulae with repeaters on them in frontier systems.

Working a guarded shell. Repeaters respawn, so clearing a shell once is not enough. The approach that fits is a heavily tanked miner that can sit on the shell and take the reflected and direct damage, plus an escort that kills each repeater as it respawns. Each round of kills also pays out echo cells and mirror facets, so the mining trip and the repeater hunt are the same trip. Frontier systems are mostly off the lanes, so both ships usually need a pathfinder drive.

Monoliths are not a harder spire: today they are undefeatable. Every kill spawns two more, so the fight only grows, and a ship in that fight cannot flee it. One player fought Monoliths continuously for eight hours, destroyed 354, and was lost (player-reported).

Treat any system where a Monolith has been seen as closed, even if it holds seam glass. Only slivers, panes and spires simply respawn; only Monoliths multiply.

Found one end from the system it will stand in, naming the system the other end will stand in as far_system. The field is far_system: system_id is rejected with invalid_payload (measured). The site anchors at its own new point of interest and your ship is docked there.

From the server's reply (measured):

Deposit construction materials into faction storage here; each facility cycle builds a share of the current phase and pays labor from your faction treasury. Use 'station' info to see the phase and what it still needs. Found the far end the same way, naming this system as its far_system. The gate opens when both ends are built and both hold upkeep.

So a gate is two construction sites, one at each end, each fed from its own faction storage, each paying labor from the treasury while it builds. station info at the site shows a gate block: the phase number and name, phases_total (4), phase_cycles, phase_labor_per_cycle and phase_materials_left (measured).

Guard the site from the moment it exists. It shows up in get_system for everyone in the system, it has no shield and no weapons, and in a lawless system nobody else will defend it. A site has been destroyed less than two hours after founding (measured): the 2,500,000 cr fee was lost with it. Found the site with an escort already in the system, or pick an end where you can keep ships on station for the whole build, about a week. Hold the construction materials elsewhere until the site is guarded.

Phase 1, Base platform, takes 500 Hull Plating, 300 Reinforced Bulkhead and 300 Structural Frame, runs 100 cycles and pays 37,000 cr of labor per cycle: 3.7M cr for the phase, about 3.2M cr a day while it builds, and that is for one end. Have the treasury ready before materials go in.

Phase 2, Infrastructure, takes 300 Circuit Board, 60 Control Node, 150 Coolant Loop and 2 Station Reactor. Phase 3, Gate frame, takes 90 Docking Ring, 20 Fold Lattice and 220 Reinforced Frame. Phase 4 takes the gate parts: 20 more Fold Lattice, 10 Gate Regulator, the end's one Lane Anchor, 60 Quantum Control Core and 1 Quantum Entangler (player-reported, from station info at another builder's site). So each end takes 40 Fold Lattice, 10 Gate Regulators and 1 Lane Anchor; a whole gate twice that.

Labor for the later phases is lower: 6,000, 25,000 and 18,000 cr per cycle for phases 2, 3 and 4, which run 150, 150 and 200 cycles (player-reported). Phase by phase that is 3.7M, 0.9M, 3.75M and 3.6M: about 11.95M cr of labor per end over 600 cycles, about seven days if materials never run short, and about 23.9M cr for the whole gate, before materials and the two 2.5M founding fees. The gate block also carries a toll (0 at founding) and a faction_only flag.

One pair at a time. While your faction has a gate under construction, founding another site fails with build_failed: your faction is already building a gate (<name>) — finish that pair before starting another (player-reported, seen during phases 2 and 3). Pick the far system before you pay the 2.5M founding fee: the far end has to be the system you named, and no second pair can start until this one is finished.

These rates come from another player's upkeep panel for one end of an open gate, a facility cycle being about 100 ticks (86.4 a day). Their panel notes the rates are read from an API change, not reported by the game, so treat them as a guide. Upkeep is drawn from the faction storage at that end, and the gate opens only while both ends hold upkeep, so budget for two ends: roughly 864 Fuel Cells, 86 Circuit Boards, 35 Coolant Loops, 17 Fold Shims and about 104,000 cr of labor a day for the pair.

Shims. The fold wears through its Fold Shims, so a gate keeps eating Annealed Seam Glass and Circuit Board for as long as it stands.

Entanglers. Quantum Entanglers sit in a gate's storage as upkeep (player-reported; the mission text agrees). Sometimes one goes wrong: the logs call it incoherent. Incoherent Entanglers have been found in gate storage, including one in an unfinished gate's storage on its inaccessible side (player-reported). First Step Memorial Station's mission It Hums asks for one, and its text now explains how they come about.
