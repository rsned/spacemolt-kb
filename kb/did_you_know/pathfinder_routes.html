Pathfinder Drive Routes

The Pathfinder Drive lets a pilot hyper-jump straight along a heading instead of hopping the gate network — until the ray passes within 100 GU of a system's center, where it drops out. These are the extremes of direct travel across all 505 systems.

Every system is modelled as a 100 GU disc. Aiming a jump at a bearing carves a corridor across the galaxy; the nearest disc the corridor touches is where you land. From that one idea fall the longest clean runs, the hairline-thin aiming tolerances, and the open voids at the galaxy's edge.

🕸️ Reaching the Whole Galaxy (Adding Stops)

A single jump only reaches nearby systems, but chaining clear jumps connects everything. Treating each uninterrupted jump as one leg, the entire galaxy is a single connected network — from any system you can reach all 504 others. Analysis Notes

  • Longest ≠ farthest: The most-distant pair (13,931 GU) is blocked; the longest clean jump is 11,875 GU.
  • Tolerances are directional: A→B and B→A can differ because the interrupting systems along each ray differ.
  • Open sky lives at the edge: Every wide void gap belongs to an outskirt system and points away from the crowded core.
  • The core is a cage: Grumium, well inside the disc, is the only system with no escape heading at all.
  • Travel time: One jump tick covers 10 GU in 10 seconds, so the longest jump takes over three real-time hours.
  • The galaxy is fully connected: Using only clean jumps as legs, every system can reach all 504 others — and any pair is linked in at most 4 jumps (3 stops).
  • Two stops is almost everything: One stop reaches ~61% of systems on average; two stops reaches 98.9%.
