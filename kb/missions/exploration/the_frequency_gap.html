The Frequency Gap ★★★★★★★★

Fly through the Experiment system to record The Array's raw emissions profile and reveal what the Voidborn are filtering from the Signal.

Dialog

Offer

Korin's been analyzing the relay emissions data. The crystalline substrates in those relays aren't amplifying the full Signal — they're isolating a narrow frequency band and suppressing everything outside it. The Signal isn't one transmission. It has structure. Multiple layers, multiple frequency bands. The Voidborn are selectively listening to one part and filtering out the rest. But the relay data only shows what they're keeping. To understand what they're filtering out, we need the unfiltered Signal — the raw transmission before the Collective's equipment processes it. There's only one place in the galaxy where the Signal is being received without filtration: The Array at the Experiment. The Array predates the Collective. Whatever it receives, it receives whole — the filtering happens downstream. We need you to fly through the Experiment system and let your sensors record The Array's raw emissions profile. A fly-by, nothing more.

Complete

Your sensor logs from The Array are extraordinary. That structure is broadcasting across a frequency range wider than anything we've ever measured — wider than the relay stations receive, wider than the original First Step equipment was designed to capture. It's broadcasting everything. And now we can see what the relays are doing. They're taking The Array's full output and splitting it. One frequency band passes through to the Collective's communications network. Everything outside that band is being actively suppressed. Filtered out. Blocked. The Signal has at least two layers. The Voidborn are listening to one and blocking the other. The original First Step team heard the whole transmission as a single phenomenon. They couldn't distinguish the layers. They detected 'a signal.' The Voidborn have known all along that it's two. We need to hear what they're hearing. And more importantly, we need to hear what they're blocking. Because you don't build a galaxy-spanning filter network to suppress something harmless.
