How to read these

Each demo presents a side-by-side comparison: the current method (or a naïve baseline) against the proposed technique. Sliders re-render both panels with new parameters in real time so you can see the technique behave under stress, not just in its best case.

The tier badge reflects estimated visual impact ÷ implementation effort. Tier S items are the highest-leverage changes — small code changes with dramatic visual upgrades. Tier A items require more work but unlock entire new aesthetic categories. Tier B items are polish layers that make the difference between "convincing" and "hand-painted."

01 Tier S · highest impact

Coastlines, biomes, the whole terran package

Domain warping + coastal-noise enhancement + Whittaker T×M biome lookup. Three tier-S techniques composed in one demo.

Problem Current generator thresholds fBm against ocean level. Coastlines are blobby (no fjords or fingering), have identical character at every scale, and biomes are pure latitude bands — so deserts and rainforests can't coexist at the same latitude.
Plain fBm + threshold
Current method. Blobby coast, single palette.
+ Domain warping
Quilez warp. Fjord-finger coastlines.
+ Coastal noise + Whittaker
Archipelagos at sea level, T×M biomes.
Sea level0.50
Warp strength0.60
Seed7
What to look for Drop warp strength to 0 and coastlines collapse to the blobby threshold look. Push past 0.8 and fjord fingering plus outlying island groups appear with no extra noise octaves. The third panel adds the (1−e⁴) coastal-noise bell so detail concentrates only at sea level — interior land and deep ocean stay smooth, archipelagos appear at every zoom for free.
Implementation Domain warping is one Quilez pass: q = p + amp · vec3(noise(p+a), noise(p+b), noise(p+c)). Whittaker biome lookup is a 4×4 grid of base colors interpolated bilinearly in (T, M). Coastal noise is (1 − e⁴) as a bell to mask high-frequency detail near sea level. ~150 lines of Go combined.

05 Tier S · highest impact

OkLab biome blending

Replace component-wise RGB color interpolation with OkLab perceptual interpolation. ~30 lines of code, single biggest aesthetic win in the entire report.

Problem Biome color lookup currently interpolates in RGB space. This passes through dingy gray midpoints between saturated colors — desert→forest goes through olive-brown muck, lava→ice through dead purple. Every biome boundary is a visible muddy zone where neither side reads as itself.
Whittaker biomes, RGB lerp
Boundaries pass through muddy midpoints.
Whittaker biomes, OkLab lerp
Edges curve through clean saturated tones.
What to look for The desert↔forest strip is the cleanest demonstration — RGB passes through olive-brown around 50%, OkLab curves through a livelier mustard-yellow. Lava↔ice is most dramatic; RGB goes dead gray-purple, OkLab stays saturated. On the planet patches, biome boundary zones are noticeably crisper in OkLab.
Implementation Use lucasb-eyer/go-colorful's BlendOklab wherever your Whittaker biome lookup currently does component-wise RGB lerp. The transform is sRGB → linear → LMS via 3×3 matrix → cube root → OkLab via second 3×3, reversed for output. ~30 LOC inline if you don't want the dependency.

07 Tier B

Cloud layer with Coriolis cyclones

Latitude-banded coverage, domain-warped fBm density, log-spiral cyclonic vortices with hemisphere-flipping rotation.

Problem Bare biome maps look like Cassini flyby stills, not photos of inhabited Earth-like worlds. The atmospheric layer is what gives a planet visual depth, soft edges, and the unmistakable "real planet" feeling. Latitude bands of coverage (ITCZ, subtropical highs, storm tracks, polar haze) plus cyclonic spirals at storm centers reproduce most Earth-from-orbit photographs.

08 Tier A

Continentality via distance-to-coast

BFS (or JFA) distance field from ocean inland, modulating moisture as M = M_base · exp(−D / L_cont). Foundation for many downstream effects.

10 Tier B

Orographic rain shadow

Latitude-banded prevailing winds (trades, westerlies, polar easterlies) with carry sweep that picks up moisture over ocean and drops it on windward slopes.

Problem Mountains have no climate effect. In reality, prevailing winds picking up moisture over ocean, dropping it on the windward side of mountains, and leaving a desert in the lee is responsible for the Atacama, the Gobi, the Patagonian steppe, and most distinct biome boundaries on Earth. Without this, deserts and forests have no relationship to terrain.
Random moisture
No relationship to terrain or wind direction.
+ Prevailing wind rain shadow
Carry sweep through latitude-banded winds.

Wind strength1.00
Sea level0.48
What to look for In the tropics (mid-canvas excluding equator) trade winds blow east-to-west: windward = right, lee desert = left. In the mid-latitudes the westerlies blow west-to-east: the asymmetry flips. The same mountain creates desert on opposite sides in different bands. This is exactly why the Atacama (mid-latitude lee) and the Sahara (trade-wind interior) sit on different sides of their respective continents.
